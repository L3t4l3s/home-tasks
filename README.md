## What is Home Tasks?

Home Tasks turns Home Assistant into the place where your household keeps its to-dos. It has two parts that work together:

- 🧩 **An integration** that keeps your lists in Home Assistant. Every list is a regular `todo.*` entity with a calendar, sensors, events and services — so tasks can drive automations, show up on your phone and be added by voice.
- 🖼️ **A dashboard card** to work with them: a clean list or a picture grid, several lists side by side, drag & drop, filters per person and tag, and every detail of a task one tap away.

Already using **Todoist, Google Tasks, Nextcloud, Bring** or another to-do app? Link those lists, and they get the same card and the same features — while your family keeps using the app they know.

**Why Home Tasks?**

- 🔁 **Recurring tasks done right** — "every 2nd Saturday", "Mon–Fri" or "3 days after I last did it", and they come back on their own.
- 👨‍👩‍👧 **Made for families** — assign tasks to people, filter by person, and let reminders find the right one.
- 🤖 **Ready for automations** — events when tasks are due, overdue or completed, plus services, a calendar and sensors for every list.
- 🏠 **Local** — your lists live in Home Assistant. Nothing leaves the house unless you link a cloud list or ask AI for a picture.

## Features

### Tasks

- **Due date and time**, with overdue highlighting
- **Priority** — low, medium, high
- **Several assignees** — any Home Assistant person
- **Tags**, **notes** and **sub-tasks** with a progress bar
- **Reminders** — up to 5 per task, as Home Assistant events
- **Picture** — picked from your media or generated by AI
- **Recurring tasks** — every N hours to years, on weekdays, on a day of the month, "the 2nd Saturday" or a yearly date; with a start date, end date or number of times
  - counts from when you did it or keeps to its due dates — your choice
  - can reopen early, e.g. the Saturday shop comes back on Monday
  - survives Home Assistant's *clear completed*; to end it, delete it in the card or switch recurrence off
- **History** — every change with who made it and when

### Dashboard card

- **List or tile view** per list — rows with every detail one tap away, or a picture grid where you tap a tile to tick it off
- **Several lists side by side** in one card, e.g. as a Kanban board
- **Sections** — group a list under headers like store aisles, rooms or project phases; every dashboard shows the same ones
- **Drag & drop** — reorder, sort into sections, or move tasks to another list
- **Filter and sort** — open/done/due soon, by date, priority, title or person; tap a tag or person chip to filter
- **Task details inline** — edit every field; fold the ones you rarely need; one-tap due dates (today, tomorrow, in 1 week …)
- **Voice input** — dictate a task with the mic button (Home Assistant Assist, or the browser's speech recognition)
- **Pictures and AI** — a picture per task from your media, or drawn by AI from its title and reused for every task with that title ([setup](#task-pictures-and-ai-generation))
- **Linked to-do lists** — Todoist, Google Tasks, Nextcloud/CalDAV, Bring and more in the same card, with every Home Tasks feature ([providers](#linked-lists))
- **Duplicate, move, auto-delete completed** — per task or per list
- **Compact mode**, smooth animations, and a **visual editor** for all of it

### Home Assistant integration

- **7 automation events** — created, completed, reopened, due, overdue, assigned, reminder ([Events](#events))
- **Services** — add (also from a spoken sentence), update, move, complete, reopen, assign and read tasks ([Services](#services))
- **[View Assist](#view-assist)** — a ready-made view and blueprints for voice satellites with a screen

### Languages

English · German · French · Spanish · Portuguese · Brazilian Portuguese · Italian · Dutch · Polish · Swedish · Danish · Norwegian · Finnish · Czech · Russian · Hungarian

## Entities

For each list — native and linked — the integration creates:

| Entity | What it shows |
|--------|---------------|
| `todo.{list_name}` | The list as a standard to-do entity (Companion App, Assist, Apple Watch, every to-do card). Native lists only — a linked list already has its provider's |
| `calendar.{list_name}_calendar` | Tasks with a due date as events — all-day, or one hour at the due time. Recurring tasks appear on every occurrence |
| `sensor.{list_name}_open_tasks` | Number of open tasks. Attributes: `open_task_titles`, `overdue_count`, `total_tasks` |
| `binary_sensor.{list_name}_overdue` | `on` while a task is overdue. Attributes: `overdue_tasks`, `overdue_count` |

On a linked list, the sensors are `unavailable` while the provider is, so an outage never reads as an empty list.

## Installation

### Marketplace (Home Assistant 2026.11 and newer, recommended)

Home Tasks is in the catalog of the Marketplace that is built into Home Assistant.

1. Go to **Settings** → **Marketplace** and search for **Home Tasks**
2. Click **Download**
3. Set it up as described under [Setup](#setup) — a first install needs no restart; after an update, Home Assistant offers the restart as a repair

An existing HACS installation carries over when you update to 2026.11: Home Tasks stays installed and keeps getting its updates through the Marketplace.

### HACS (Home Assistant before 2026.11)

Home Tasks is available in the HACS default repository.

[![Open your Home Assistant instance and open the Home Tasks repository inside the Home Assistant Community Store.](https://my.home-assistant.io/badges/hacs_repository.svg)](https://my.home-assistant.io/redirect/hacs_repository/?owner=L3t4l3s&repository=home-tasks&category=integration)

1. Click the button above — or open HACS and search for **Home Tasks**
2. Click **Download**
3. Restart Home Assistant

### Manual

1. Copy the `custom_components/home_tasks` folder into your `config/custom_components/` directory
2. Restart Home Assistant

## Setup

Every list is set up as its own entry of the integration. The dashboard card is registered automatically — just add it to your dashboard.

### Native Lists

1. Go to **Settings** → **Devices & Services** → **Add Integration**
2. Search for **Home Tasks**
3. Click **Add Service**
4. Choose **Create a new task list**
5. Enter a name for your list
6. Repeat for additional lists

### Linked Lists

1. Set up the external provider's HA integration first (e.g. CalDAV, Google Tasks, Todoist)
2. Go to **Settings** → **Devices & Services** → **Add Integration**
3. Search for **Home Tasks**
4. Click **Add Service**
5. Choose **Link an external todo list**
6. Select the todo entity from the dropdown
7. The external list is now available in the card editor

**Supported providers**

| Provider | Synced with the provider |
|----------|--------------------------|
| **[Todoist](https://www.home-assistant.io/integrations/todoist/)** | Nearly everything: title, status, notes, due date & time, priority, labels, order, sub-tasks, assignee, recurrence, reminders |
| **[CalDAV](https://www.home-assistant.io/integrations/caldav/)** (Nextcloud, …) | Title, status, notes, due date & time |
| **[Google Tasks](https://www.home-assistant.io/integrations/google_tasks/)** | Title, status, notes, due date, order |
| **[Local To-do](https://www.home-assistant.io/integrations/local_todo/)** | Title, status, notes, due date |
| **[Bring!](https://www.home-assistant.io/integrations/bring/)** | Title, status, notes |
| **[Shopping List](https://www.home-assistant.io/integrations/shopping_list/)** | Title, status |

Anything a provider can't store — priority, tags, sub-tasks, reminders, recurrence, pictures — Home Tasks keeps locally, so **every feature works on every list**, including events, calendar and sensors. Any other integration with standard `todo.*` entities should work too.

- **Recurrence** runs in Home Tasks for providers that have none; Todoist keeps its own.
- **Google Tasks** — Google's API has no due times and no recurrence ([open issue](https://issuetracker.google.com/issues/36759725)); the recurrence in Google's web UI never reaches the API. Use Home Tasks' recurrence instead.
- **Todoist** — Home Tasks talks to the Todoist API directly, with the token of your existing Todoist integration; no extra setup. Not synced: comments, attachments, Todoist sections, task duration, label colors, deadlines, favorites and saved filters.

### Task pictures and AI generation

Turn pictures on per list with `show_images: true` (card editor → *Images*). Then the task details offer:

- **Pick** — any file from your Home Assistant `media/` sources
- **Generate** — a picture from the task title, by an [`ai_task`](https://www.home-assistant.io/integrations/ai_task/) entity
- **Remove** — the × on the thumbnail

To generate pictures, set the AI entity in the card editor under *Images*, or in YAML:

```yaml
type: custom:home-tasks-card
image_generation:
  entity_id: ai_task.openai          # any ai_task.* entity (OpenAI, Gemini, …)
  prompt_prefix: "Minimalist icon of" # optional, prepended to every prompt
columns:
  - list_id: "your-list-id"
    show_images: true
    auto_generate_image: true        # generate automatically on task creation
```

> The main **Model** of your AI entity must be a text/vision model; the actual image model is configured in the AI integration's *image generation* option. `auto_generate_image` only runs when `show_images` is on **and** an `image_generation` entity is set.

**Automatic pictures** — with `auto_generate_image`, the integration draws a picture for every new task of the list on its own, however it was added (voice, automation, recurrence), no open dashboard needed. A failed generation is not retried; the task shows a placeholder, and the generate button tries again. The card editor's *Images* section shows the queue.

Pictures are stored in `config/www/home_tasks/` and reused for every task with the same title, in every list. A list can opt out under *Images* → **Share images with other lists**, e.g. when each child has their own list with their own pictures.

### Column option reference

Each entry in `columns` accepts the following options.

| Option | Default | Description |
|--------|---------|-------------|
| `list_id` | — | The native list to display (use this **or** `entity_id`) |
| `entity_id` | — | An external todo entity to display (use this **or** `list_id`) |
| `title` | List name | Custom column title |
| `icon` | — | MDI icon shown next to the column title (e.g. `mdi:home`) |
| `view_mode` | `list` | Layout: `list` (detailed rows) or `tiles` (image grid) |
| `list_columns` | `1` | List view: lay a list's tasks out in up to 4 columns (section headers stay full width, an opened task spans the row). Columns stay at least `list_column_min_width` wide (default 160px — two fit on a phone), so narrow screens fall back to fewer |
| `list_column_min_width` | `160` | List view with `list_columns`: minimum column width in px (80–600) before the card drops to fewer columns |
| `tile_size` | `medium` | Tiles view: `small` (80px), `medium` (the built-in size: 140px, 100px with `compact`), `large` (200px), or a minimum tile width in px (60–400) |
| `default_filter` | `all` | Initial filter: `all`, `open`, `done`, or `due_soon` |
| `default_sort` | `manual` | Initial sort: `manual`, `due`, `priority`, `title`, or `person` |
| `overdue_first` | `false` | Open overdue tasks lead the list — oldest due date first — whatever the sort; completed tasks stay at the bottom. Works within each section. With manual sort, an overdue task stays on top even when dragged; the position you drag it to applies once it's no longer overdue |
| `compact` | `false` | Compact mode for denser task rows |
| `auto_delete_completed` | `false` | Automatically delete completed tasks |
| `confirm_complete` | `false` | Ask for confirmation before marking a task as completed (guards against accidental taps on touch devices) |
| `tap_to_complete` | `false` | List view: tapping anywhere on a task row completes it (or reopens a done one), like a tile; the chevron on the right opens the details, where the title is edited. Works together with `confirm_complete`. Tiles always behave this way |
| `max_height` | — | Maximum height of the task list in px; the list scrolls internally while title, add-task row and filters stay fixed (e.g. `max_height: 350`). Unset/0 = grows with content. In a *sections* dashboard prefer the card's **Layout** tab: a fixed number of rows makes the card fill exactly that height and scroll its list the same way |
| **Header / chrome** | | |
| `show_title` | `true` | Show/hide the column title |
| `show_progress` | `true` | Show/hide the task progress counter |
| `show_add_task` | `true` | Show/hide the "add task" input row (set `false` for a read-only display) |
| `show_add_due` | `false` | Show a due date (+ time) row under the add-task input so a due date can be set while creating the task; leave it empty to create without one. Hidden for external lists whose provider can't store due dates |
| `show_add_details` | `false` | A second button (✎) next to + that opens a **draft** under the add field (also with the field still empty) with the same detail fields as a task (in your `detail_order`, honouring the `show_*` switches), filled with the list's defaults and the add row's due date. While it is open, the add field is the draft's title (no search) and the add row's due-date inputs give way to the draft's own. Nothing is created until *Add*, + or Enter; *Cancel*, Escape or ✎ again closes it and keeps the title, the field's clear × discards it. Without a draft, + and Enter still quick-add. (The add field also has a clear × while it holds text.) |
| `show_voice` | `true` | Show/hide the voice-input mic button on the add-task row |
| `show_task_search` | `true` | Live-search while typing (or dictating) in the add-task row: the column shows matching tasks — open and completed, across all sections — so you spot a task you already have instead of adding a duplicate. Escape, adding the task, or ticking off a match ends the search |
| `show_sort` | `true` | Show/hide the sort button |
| `show_filters` | `true` | Show/hide the All / Open / Done filter buttons |
| `show_tag_chips` | `true` | Show/hide the tag filter chips in the header |
| `person_filter` | `name` | How the person filter row in the header shows people: `off`, `picture` (profile picture only), `name`, `both`. Replaces `show_person_chips` |
| `show_due_soon_filter` | `false` | Enable the "Due Soon" filter button |
| `due_soon_days` | `7` | Days ahead for the "Due Soon" filter (0–90, 0 = due today only) |
| `hide_overdue` | `false` | Hide overdue tasks in the "Due Soon" filter (overdue shown by default) |
| **Per-task fields** | | |
| `show_images` | `false` | Show task images (tile background / list thumbnail) |
| `auto_generate_image` | `false` | Auto-generate an image with AI when a task is created (needs `show_images` + a card-level `image_generation` entity). Works on native and external lists; on an external list the image follows once the provider has assigned the task its id |
| `show_tile_title` | `true` | (Tiles view) Show the title overlay on each tile |
| `show_notes` | `true` | Show/hide the notes field |
| `show_sub_tasks` | `true` | Show/hide sub-tasks |
| `show_assigned_person` | `true` | Show/hide person assignment |
| `hidden_persons` | — | List of `person.*` entities not offered for assignment in this card (e.g. a system or visiting person). Someone already assigned to a task stays visible. Set in the visual editor under *Selectable people*: only the highlighted people are offered |

### Card-level option reference

These options live at the **root** of the card config, not inside a column.

| Option | Default | Description |
|--------|---------|-------------|
| `columns` | — | List of column configs (required) |
| `title` | — | Optional card title shown above the columns |
| `image_generation.entity_id` | — | An `ai_task.*` entity used to generate task images |
| `image_generation.prompt_prefix` | — | Text prepended to every AI image prompt (e.g. `Minimalist icon of`) |
| `grid_options` | — | Standard HA grid sizing (e.g. `columns: 36`, `rows: auto`) |

The old flat format (`list_id` at root level) is still supported and migrated automatically.

### List defaults

Each list — native or linked — can preset new tasks (card editor → *Defaults*): assignees, reminders, tags, priority and section. They apply however a task is created: card, voice, service or `todo.add_item`; values given when creating it win. The same place sets:

- **Fixed tags** — always suggested in the tag input, and a near-miss typo (`kitchn`) offers the fixed tag instead
- **Reopen early** — a list-wide lead for recurring tasks ([Tasks](#tasks))

### Styling

**card-mod** works on this card — a plain `style:` string is applied inside the card's shadow DOM, so you can target its classes directly:

```yaml
type: custom:home-tasks-card
columns:
  - list_id: ...
card_mod:
  style: |
    .task-title { font-size: 16px !important; }
```

For a theme-wide setting, the task title also exposes CSS custom properties that you can set in a [Home Assistant theme](https://www.home-assistant.io/integrations/frontend/#defining-themes) — or per card via card-mod with `:host { --ht-task-title-font-size: 16px; }` (fallbacks equal the defaults):

| Variable | Default |
|----------|---------|
| `--ht-task-title-font-family` | inherited |
| `--ht-task-title-font-size` | `14px` |
| `--ht-task-title-font-weight` | inherited |
| `--ht-task-title-color` | theme text color |

```yaml
# themes.yaml
my_theme:
  ht-task-title-font-family: "'Comic Neue', cursive"
  ht-task-title-font-size: 16px
```

## Automations

### Events

| Event | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| `home_tasks_task_created` | Fired when a task is created |
| `home_tasks_task_completed` | Fired when a task is marked as done |
| `home_tasks_task_due` | Fired when a task's due date is today (once per day) |
| `home_tasks_task_overdue` | Fired when a task is past its due date (once per day) |
| `home_tasks_task_assigned` | Fired when a task's assignees change — including tasks *created* with an assignee (explicitly, via the list default, or by duplicating for another person); `previous_person` is `null` in that case. Also carries `previous_persons` (everyone assigned before) and `added_persons` (the people new to the task — the ones to notify). It also fires when only a co-assignee is added or removed and `assigned_person` stays the same, so an automation that notifies on this event should send to `added_persons`, not to `assigned_person` |
| `home_tasks_task_reopened` | Fired when a task is reopened (manually or by recurrence) |
| `home_tasks_task_reminder` | Fired at the configured offset before a task's due time |

All events include: `entry_id`, `task_id`, `task_title`, `list_name`, and (if set) `assigned_person` (the primary assignee), `assigned_persons` (everyone assigned, primary first), `due_date`, `due_time`, `priority`, `notes` (truncated to 255 chars), `tags`.
Events for external lists additionally include `entity_id` (the external todo entity).
The `home_tasks_task_reminder` event additionally includes `reminder_offset_minutes`.
The `home_tasks_task_completed` event additionally includes who ticked the task off, when a user did — from the card, the HA todo entity's actions (Companion App, `todo.update_item`) or a service called by a user: `completed_by` (the user's name, as in the task history), `completed_by_user_id`, and `completed_by_person` (the `person.*` linked to that user). The event runs in that user's context, so the logbook names them too. Completions by automations, by the recurrence, in a provider's own app or by an Assist voice command (Home Assistant's todo intents don't pass on who spoke) carry none of these fields.

### Services

Every task service works on a **linked list** too: name it with `list_name` as usual, or give its todo entity as `entity_id`. The provider stores what it can, Home Tasks keeps the rest.

#### `home_tasks.add_task_from_text`

Create a task from a spoken-style sentence — made for voice (see the
[add-by-voice blueprint](docs/view-assist/README.md#4-add-tasks-by-voice-optional)).
Clauses at the end of the text fill the fields, in any order; everything else
is the title. A clause only counts when it really parses, so "look for the
keys" stays a title.

| Clause | Examples (English / German) |
|--------|-----------------------------|
| Person — only people that exist, full or unique first name | `for Anna` / `für Anna` |
| Priority | `with high priority`, `low priority`, `priority high` / `mit hoher Priorität`, `Priorität hoch` |
| Due date | `today`, `tomorrow`, `the day after tomorrow`, `due Friday`, `next Monday`, `in 3 days`, `in two weeks`, `in a month`, `in a year`, `on 5 October` / `heute`, `morgen`, `übermorgen`, `am Freitag`, `nächsten Montag`, `in drei Tagen`, `in zwei Monaten`, `in einem Jahr`, `am 5. Oktober`, `bis zum 5.10.` |
| Time (a time alone means today, or tomorrow if already past) | `at 5 pm`, `at 17:30`, `at noon` / `um 17 Uhr`, `um 9:30` |

A weekday on its own ("due Friday", "am Freitag") is the next one, today
included; "next Friday" is never today. Numbers can be digits or words up
to 999 ("in sixty days", "in einundzwanzig Tagen"); "in a month" keeps the
day of the month, or takes the month's last day when it has fewer.

| Field | Required | Description |
|-------|----------|-------------|
| `list_name` / `entry_id` / `entity_id` | * | The list, as for `add_task` |
| `text` | yes | The sentence, without the command in front of it |
| `language` | no | `en` or `de`; defaults to the Home Assistant language (other languages are read with the English words) |

Returns (with `response_variable`) `task_id`, `list_name`, `title`,
`assigned_person`, `person_name`, `priority`, `due_date`, `due_time`,
`language` and `details` — the understood fields as a phrase for a spoken
answer, e.g. `for Anna, high priority, due Friday 2 October at 17:00`.

#### `home_tasks.get_tasks`

Read a list's tasks with every Home Tasks field — what `todo.get_items` can't
return, since Home Assistant's todo items have no assignee or tags. Call it
with `response_variable`.

| Field | Required | Description |
|-------|----------|-------------|
| `list_name` / `entry_id` / `entity_id` | * | The list, as for `add_task` |
| `status` | no | `open` (default), `completed` or `all` |
| `assigned_person` | no | Only tasks this person is one of the assignees of (e.g. `person.anna`) |
| `tag` | no | Only tasks with this tag (case-insensitive) |
| `due` | no | `today`, `overdue` or `today_or_overdue` |

Returns `list_name`, the list's fixed tags as `tag_catalog`, and `tasks`, in the card's order; each task has `id`,
`title`, `completed`, `completed_at`, `due_date`, `due_time`,
`assigned_person`, `tags`, `priority`, `notes`, `section_id`, `sub_items`
(`id`, `title`, `completed`) and `recurrence_enabled`.

A badge count of *my* tasks due today, as a trigger-based template sensor:

```yaml
template:
  - trigger:
      - trigger: time_pattern
        minutes: /5
      - trigger: event
        event_type: home_tasks_task_completed
    action:
      - action: home_tasks.get_tasks
        data:
          list_name: Household
          due: today
          assigned_person: person.anna
        response_variable: mine
    sensor:
      - name: Anna due today
        state: "{{ mine.tasks | count }}"
```

#### Recurrence fields

`add_task` and `update_task` take the recurrence settings of the card's task
editor. Name only what you want set: the rest stays as it is, and settings
that belong to another unit are cleared, as in the card. Naming any of them
makes the task recurring; `recurrence_enabled: false` switches it off.

| Field | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| `recurrence_unit` | `hours`, `days`, `weeks`, `months` or `years` — needed when the task has never recurred |
| `recurrence_value` | Every how many units (`2` + `weeks` = every 2 weeks) |
| `recurrence_weekdays` | Weekly on these days, as a list or comma-separated (`mon` … `sun`); monthly, the one day for `recurrence_nth_week`. `[]` ends a weekday pattern |
| `recurrence_day_of_month` | Monthly on this day (`1`–`31` or `last`) |
| `recurrence_nth_week` | Monthly on the nth weekday (`1`–`4` or `last`), with one day in `recurrence_weekdays` |
| `recurrence_anniversary` | Yearly on this date (`MM-DD`) |
| `recurrence_anchor` | Without a fixed day: count from the `completion` (default) or the `due` date |
| `recurrence_start_date` / `recurrence_time` | Start date, and the time of day it becomes due |
| `recurrence_end_date` / `recurrence_max_count` | End after a date, or after a number of repetitions (one of them; empty = no end) |
| `recurrence_reopen_lead` | [Reopen early](#list-defaults), in minutes, shorter than the interval — Home Tasks lists only. Left out, a task that starts recurring takes the list's default |
| `recurrence_enabled` | `false` switches recurrence off, keeping its settings for later |

A combination the card wouldn't offer — a day of month on a weekly task, a
lead as long as the interval — fails the call and changes nothing. On a
Todoist list the provider repeats the task itself, so there is no `due`
anchor and no fixed day on an every-2-months series.

```yaml
action: home_tasks.add_task
data:
  list_name: Household
  title: Bins out
  recurrence_unit: weeks
  recurrence_weekdays: [mon, thu]
  recurrence_time: "07:00"
  section: Outside
```

#### `home_tasks.add_sub_task` / `update_sub_task` / `remove_sub_task`

Manage a task's sub-tasks, the same as the card does. On a linked Todoist
list they are Todoist's own sub-tasks; on other linked lists Home Tasks keeps
them. `add_sub_task` returns (with `response_variable`) `task_id` and the new
`sub_task_id`.

| Field | Required | Description |
|-------|----------|-------------|
| `list_name` / `entry_id` / `entity_id` | * | The list, as for `add_task` |
| `task_title` | ** | Title of the task |
| `task_id` | ** | ID of the task |
| `title` | add: yes, update: no | The sub-task's title; for `update_sub_task` its new title |
| `sub_task_title` | update/remove | Current title of the sub-task (case-insensitive) |
| `sub_task_id` | update/remove | ID of the sub-task, as `get_tasks` returns it (alternative to `sub_task_title`) |
| `completed` | no | `update_sub_task` only: `true` checks it off, `false` unchecks it |

When two sub-tasks share a title, `update_sub_task` changes the one not yet in
the requested state — ticking "Milk" twice ticks both.

```yaml
- action: home_tasks.update_sub_task
  data:
    list_name: Household
    task_title: Groceries
    sub_task_title: Milk
    completed: true
```

*\* Either `list_name` or `entry_id`. \*\* See individual service descriptions for required combinations.*

### Example Automations

Count who gets things done — one [counter](https://www.home-assistant.io/integrations/counter/) helper per person (`counter.done_kevin`, `counter.done_anna`, …), bumped on every completion:

```yaml
automation:
  - alias: "Home Tasks: count completions per person"
    trigger:
      - platform: event
        event_type: home_tasks_task_completed
    condition:
      - "{{ trigger.event.data.completed_by_person is defined }}"
    action:
      - service: counter.increment
        target:
          entity_id: "counter.done_{{ trigger.event.data.completed_by_person.split('.')[1] }}"
```

Reset the counters weekly, feed them into a statistics card, or skip the
counters and chart the events straight from the logbook.

Send a notification when a reminder fires:

```yaml
automation:
  - alias: "Home Tasks: Reminder notification"
    trigger:
      - platform: event
        event_type: home_tasks_task_reminder
    action:
      - service: notify.mobile_app
        data:
          title: "Task reminder"
          message: "{{ trigger.event.data.task_title }} is due soon"
```

Reopen morning chores when a child arrives home:

```yaml
automation:
  - alias: "Reopen morning tasks for Ben"
    trigger:
      - platform: state
        entity_id: person.ben
        to: "home"
    action:
      - service: home_tasks.reopen_task
        data:
          list_name: "Kids Chores"
          assigned_person: person.ben
          tag: "morning"
```

Complete all weekend tasks on Monday morning:

```yaml
automation:
  - alias: "Complete weekend tasks"
    trigger:
      - platform: time
        at: "08:00:00"
    condition:
      - condition: time
        weekday: [mon]
    action:
      - service: home_tasks.complete_task
        data:
          list_name: "Household"
          tag: "weekend"
```

## View Assist

[View Assist](https://dinki.github.io/View-Assist/) turns tablets and old smart
displays into voice satellites with a screen. [`docs/view-assist/`](docs/view-assist/)
ships the pieces to put a Home Tasks list on one:

- **A view** — the full Home Tasks card as a View Assist panel view at `/view-assist/hometasks`, with satellite-friendly defaults (compact rows, confirm-before-complete, open tasks only, sorted by due date). A second variant picks the list per satellite at runtime.
- **A blueprint** — "show me my task list" makes the satellite say how many tasks are open and open the view. Sentences and spoken responses are configurable.
- **A reminders blueprint** — reminders and overdue tasks are announced on the satellites, open the view and leave a status-bar icon until the task is done. Satellites are chosen like in View Assist's own blueprints (all, specific, or a template — e.g. by assigned person or the satellite used last), with quiet hours and do-not-disturb respected.
- **An add-by-voice blueprint** — "add task pay the bill for Anna with high priority due Friday at 5 pm" creates the task with all those fields and says what it added. Works on any Assist device, View Assist optional; English and German built in.

All of them are copy-and-install files, so a Home Tasks update never overwrites
your customised view or blueprints. Installation, how to pin a specific list,
choosing satellites for reminders, German sentences for the voice blueprint and
tuning the view for 800×480 screens: **[docs/view-assist/README.md](docs/view-assist/README.md)**.

Home Tasks lists are ordinary `todo.*` entities, so View Assist's built-in
**list** view and its **List Management** blueprint work with them out of the
box too — the view above just shows the real card instead of a plain todo list.

### Maintaining translations

`custom_components/home_tasks/ui_translations.json` is the shared source for card,
editor, and calendar description text. After changing it, run `npm run build:i18n`
and commit the generated `home-tasks-card.js` too. The distributed card embeds
these strings so it does not need a separate network request. The backend loads
the same JSON once in an executor during setup. Translation tests check that the
embedded card data and JSON remain identical. HA integration setup translations
stay in `translations/`, following Home Assistant's config-flow schema.
